#+TITLE: Plan C Spike — Cost of Loading GeoTIFF / LCP Landscapes
#+AUTHOR: RJ Sheperd
#+DATE: 2026-10-15

Scope: the *ingest* question only — what does it cost to get FlamMap
landscape data from a file into a GPU-ready buffer, without GDAL? The
fire-behavior comparison half of Plan C ([[file:../../FLAMMAP_WEBGPU_PLANS.org][FLAMMAP_WEBGPU_PLANS.org]] §5) is
not addressed here.

Answer: *~190 ms uncompressed, ~400–435 ms with DEFLATE, single-threaded,
for a 4096² landscape* (16.8M cells, ~123 km square at 30 m), producing
128 MB of packed cells. Ingest is not the bottleneck, and no GDAL is
required.

*Headline from the compression round (§Compression): shipping a
predictor-2 compressed source raster and packing client-side beats
shipping pre-packed cells — 17.9 MB versus 44.0 MB on the wire.* That
inverts the network half of the research brief's ADR-02.

* Files

| =lcp.mjs= | Dependency-free reader: raw =.lcp=, LCP-as-GeoTIFF (stripped + tiled), DEFLATE/ZSTD, Predictor=2, deinterleave, 8 B/cell packing. Runs in browser or bun. |
| =decoders-node.mjs= | Host-specific decoder wiring, with the browser equivalent documented. =lcp.mjs= itself takes decoders by injection and imports nothing host-specific. |
| =bench.mjs= | Timing harness, median of 7, with projections. |
| =verify.mjs= | Regression test: every codec x predictor variant must decode byte-identically to the uncompressed baseline of the same grid, at both sample and packed-cell level. |
| =gen.mjs= | Synthetic landscape generator — codec x predictor variants, so scaling and compression are measured rather than extrapolated. |

#+BEGIN_SRC shell
bun install                       # fzstd, for the ZSTD path
bun run gen.mjs /tmp/lcp 4096 4096 --tiled --raw --compressed
bun run bench.mjs /tmp/lcp/synth_4096x4096_deflate_p2.tif
bun run bench.mjs ../../SpatialFOFEM/SampleData/Landscape/BlueMountain_LCP_FCCS.tif
#+END_SRC

* Measurements

Apple Silicon, bun 1.1.38, median of 7, single-threaded. Real sample is
=SpatialFOFEM/SampleData/Landscape/BlueMountain_LCP_FCCS.tif=; the rest are
synthetic 10-band landscapes.

| Grid | Cells | Parse (ms) | Fused pack (ms) | *Total (ms)* | Packed | Deflated |
|---+---+---+---+---+---+---|
| 482 x 554 (real) | 267 K | 0.71 | 2.28 | *2.99* | 2.0 MB | 0.56 MB (3.64:1) |
| 1024² | 1.05 M | 3.56 | 8.49 | *12.05* | 8.0 MB | 1.98 MB (4.05:1) |
| 2048² | 4.19 M | 18.46 | 41.36 | *59.82* | 32 MB | 8.21 MB (3.90:1) |
| 4096² | 16.8 M | 60.43 | 129.44 | *189.88* | 128 MB | 44.0 MB (2.91:1) |

Throughput settles at roughly *130 M cells/s* for the fused pack and
*280 M cells/s* for TIFF assembly.

* Compression

4096² (16.8M cells, 10 bands, 320 MB raw), tiled 512², compared against the
uncompressed baseline. All variants decode *byte-identical* to it, at both
sample and packed-cell level.

| Variant | File size | Ratio | Decode (ms) | Decode out MB/s | Total ingest (ms) |
|---+---+---+---+---+---|
| uncompressed | 320.0 MB | 1.00:1 | — | — | ~200 |
| deflate, predictor 1 | 51.2 MB | 6.25:1 | 242 | 1322 | ~398 |
| *deflate, predictor 2* | *17.9 MB* | *17.90:1* | *138* | *2315* | *~434* |
| zstd, predictor 1 | 39.7 MB | 8.07:1 | 2554 | 125 | ~2757 |
| zstd, predictor 2 | 19.7 MB | 16.26:1 | 695 | 460 | ~1061 |

Decode is isolated (chunk decode only, best of 5). "Total ingest" is
best-case parse+decode+predictor+fused-pack. The inverse predictor pass
costs *124.6 ms* on its own at this size (1347 M samples/s).

* Findings

** F1 — Ingest is cheap enough to ignore, and GDAL is unnecessary

190 ms for a 4096² landscape, against =jessie='s 74 ms of GPU simulation
and ~950 ms end-to-end. Ingest is a minor term. More importantly the whole
reader is *~300 lines with no dependencies* — confirming
ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW S3b.6a that GDAL-in-WASM (10–20 MB of payload) can be
avoided on the input path entirely.

** F2 — Fusing deinterleave into packing is a free 2x

The obvious two-pass structure (deinterleave to per-band planes, then pack)
costs 405 ms at 4096². Writing the packed layout *directly* from the
pixel-interleaved samples costs 190 ms — *2.13x faster, byte-identical
output*, and it avoids allocating ~335 MB of intermediate planes.

Per-band planes are never needed if the consumer is the GPU. Keep
=deinterleave()= for debugging and validation only.

** F3 — The 5:1 assumption is wrong in both directions, depending on what you compress

=wildfire-sim-research.org= flags its 5:1 ZSTD ratio as an assumption. It
turns out to matter enormously *what* is being compressed:

- Compressing the *packed 8 B/cell* output: *2.91:1* at 4096² (4.05:1 at
  1024², 3.90:1 at 2048²) — *worse* than 5:1, and degrading with area,
  because packing has already stripped the redundancy compression would
  find.
- Compressing the *source 20 B/cell int16 raster* with Predictor=2:
  *17.90:1* (deflate) or 16.26:1 (zstd) — far *better* than 5:1.

So 5:1 should not be used for sizing either pipeline. See F10 for the
consequence.

Caveat: synthetic terrain, which is smoother than reality and therefore
flatters horizontal differencing. The *mechanism* is real; the *magnitude*
needs re-measuring on a real LANDFIRE window.

** F9 — Predictor=2 is roughly CPU-neutral and a large wire win

GDAL writes =PREDICTOR=2= (horizontal differencing) for integer rasters,
and the research brief's COG profile recommends it for DEMs while noting
"predictors don't help on codes." On a *pixel-interleaved multi-band LCP
stack* it helps a great deal — 6.25:1 → 17.90:1 for deflate — because the
continuous bands (elevation, aspect) difference to near-zero and the
categorical bands (fuel model) sit in large contiguous patches that also
difference to zero.

The CPU accounting is almost a wash:

| Smaller input to decode | −104 ms (242 → 138) |
| Inverse predictor pass | +125 ms |
| *Net* | *+21 ms* |

For ~21 ms of CPU it removes *33 MB* from the wire at 4096². Clearly worth
enabling.

** F10 — Compressed source beats compressed packed cells on the wire

This is the counter-intuitive one, and it inverts the network half of the
brief's ADR-02 ("8 B/cell packed ... cuts network, decode, cache and VRAM
by ~4x"):

| 4096² delivered as | Wire bytes |
|---+---|
| packed 8 B/cell, deflated | 44.0 MB |
| *source int16 raster, deflate + predictor 2* | *17.9 MB* |

Packing to 8 bits *destroys* the smooth gradients that make differencing
effective, and bit-packing heterogeneous fields into =u32= words produces a
high-entropy mixture that compresses poorly. Compressing *before* packing
preserves per-band spatial correlation.

Same ordering at 1024² (1.12 MB vs 1.98 MB), so it is not a size artefact.

*Revised recommendation:* ship predictor-2 compressed source rasters and
pack on the client. ADR-02's packing is still correct for *VRAM* (128 MB vs
537 MB as f32 layers) and for GPU bandwidth — it is only the network claim
that inverts. The two concerns should be decoupled in any design doc.

** F11 — The deflate-vs-zstd numbers here measure implementation, not codec

DEFLATE decodes at 1.3–2.3 GB/s and ZSTD at 0.13–0.46 GB/s in this harness.
*Do not read that as zstd being slower* — zstd is normally the faster
codec. The comparison is unfair by construction:

- DEFLATE goes through =node:zlib='s =inflateSync= — *native C*.
- ZSTD goes through =fzstd= — *pure JavaScript*, and the brief already
  identifies it as the slow option (~0.25–0.34 GB/s vs ~0.9–1.45 GB/s for
  the =zstddec= WASM build).

In a browser both would be comparable: =DecompressionStream('deflate')= is
native, and a WASM zstd should land in the same 1–1.5 GB/s band. *The
codec choice should be re-benchmarked with ZSTD-WASM before it is made.*
This spike's ZSTD timings are a pessimistic floor, not a verdict.

** F12 — The reader stays browser-portable, but DEFLATE forces async

=lcp.mjs= imports nothing host-specific; decoders are injected
(=decoders-node.mjs= shows the node wiring and documents the browser
equivalent). One real consequence: the browser's
=DecompressionStream('deflate')= is *async*, while the chunk-assembly loop
is synchronous. Either the loop becomes async, or chunks are decoded up
front — which is what the brief's ADR-05 (decode in CPU workers) implies
anyway, and is the better shape regardless.

** F4 — TIFF sample data is NOT safely castable to Int16Array

Strip and tile offsets are arbitrary byte offsets and are routinely *odd* —
the real BlueMountain file puts its first strip at byte 5049.
=new Int16Array(buffer, 5049)= throws =RangeError: Byte offset is not
aligned=. So zero-copy int16 views over TIFF payloads are not generally
possible; one byte-wise copy per strip/tile into an aligned destination is
unavoidable.

This cost ~20 minutes of spike time and would have cost the same in any
implementation. It is the single most likely thing to surprise someone
writing this reader fresh.

** F5 — Tiled vs stripped does not matter for whole-file reads

At 1024², stripped 21.70 ms vs tiled 21.74 ms. Tiling matters for *HTTP
range requests* (fetching a window without the whole file), not for local
parse throughput. The research brief's tiling guidance (ADR-01, 512²
tiles) is about network access patterns, and this spike does not exercise
that — it measures the local path only.

** F6 — Band count must come from the right source, per format

A raw =.lcp= implies its band count from the header's =CrownFuels= /
=GroundFuels= flags (=21= = present): 5 basic + 3 crown + 2 ground = 10.
But the real =BlueMountain_LCP_FCCS.tif= has *9* bands, which no
combination of those flags produces — it is an FCCS variant that does not
follow strict LCP banding.

So: *trust =SamplesPerPixel= for GeoTIFF, trust the flags only for raw
=.lcp=.* A reader that assumes 10 bands, or that applies the LCP flag
convention to a GeoTIFF, will stride incorrectly and silently produce
garbage.

** F7 — The 7,316-byte LCP header is confirmed, and must be parsed field-wise

Summing =headdata= (=firemodels/lib/flammap/include/flammap3.h:190-269=)
gives *exactly 7,316 bytes*, matching the research brief. Composition: 3
longs + 4 doubles + 10 themes x 412 B + 2 longs + 4 doubles + 1 long +
2 doubles + 10 shorts + 10 x 256 B filenames + 512 B description.

But the equivalent C struct would be *7,320* bytes under natural alignment
— a =double= at offset 12 forces 4 bytes of padding after the three
leading longs. *The on-disk header is unpadded*, so it must be read
field-by-field; a packed-struct memcpy will be off by 4 bytes from the
first double onward.

** F8 — Scaling is mildly super-linear

Linear extrapolation from 1024² predicts 193 ms at 4096²; measured 190 ms
for the fused path, but the two-pass path degrades (365 ms predicted vs
405 ms measured, ~1.1x) and per-stage throughput drops as the working set
leaves cache (deinterleave 82 → 74 M cells/s from 1024² to 4096²).
Projections beyond 8192² should be treated as optimistic floors.

* Correctness

=gen.mjs= writes the same synthetic landscape three ways — raw =.lcp=,
stripped GeoTIFF, tiled GeoTIFF — and all three readers produce
*byte-identical* interleaved output. The generator deliberately places
sample data at an odd offset to reproduce F4.

=verify.mjs= additionally checks every codec x predictor variant against the
uncompressed baseline of its own grid — 10 variants across 1024² and 4096²,
all *byte-identical* at both sample and packed-cell level, which exercises
the DEFLATE path, the ZSTD path, and the Predictor=2 round trip.

#+BEGIN_SRC shell
bun run verify.mjs /tmp/lcp    # => ALL VARIANTS BYTE-IDENTICAL
#+END_SRC

The fused and two-pass packing paths are asserted byte-identical in
=bench.mjs=. (They initially differed: one rounded canopy height, the other
truncated. Quantization rounding must match exactly between any two paths
that are meant to agree — a small instance of the general risk in
ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW Q0.7.)

* What this spike does NOT establish

- *No browser measurement.* All timings are bun on the dev machine. Browser
  typed-array performance should be comparable but is unconfirmed, and
  =jessie='s harness (Playwright + =test_stress.mjs=) is the place to
  confirm it.
- *No HTTP/range-request path.* F5 explains why that is the interesting
  case for large landscapes, and it is untested here. The brief's COG
  profile, OPFS caching, and Zarr weather streaming are all unexercised.
- *LZW is still unsupported.* The reader handles compression 1 (none),
  8/32946 (DEFLATE) and 50000 (ZSTD). LZW (5) appears in older TIFFs and
  would need adding for full coverage.
- *ZSTD is measured with a pure-JS decoder.* See F11 — the numbers are a
  floor, and the codec decision needs ZSTD-WASM first.
- *No fire behavior.* Packing is validated for self-consistency, not
  against FlamMap. Whether these quantized inputs produce acceptable fire
  behavior is the other half of Plan C.
- *Synthetic compression ratios.* See F3's caveat.

* Recommended next steps

1. *DONE* — DEFLATE/ZSTD decode path with Predictor=2 (see §Compression,
   F9–F12). Decode roughly doubles ingest CPU but cuts the wire payload
   6–18x; clearly worth it.
2. *Swap =fzstd= for a WASM zstd (=zstddec=) and re-run F11* before
   choosing a codec. This is the highest-value remaining measurement.
3. Re-run F3/F9/F10 against a *real LANDFIRE window* rather than synthetic
   terrain. F10 in particular is a design-changing claim resting on
   synthetic data.
4. Port =bench.mjs= into =jessie='s Playwright harness for in-browser
   numbers, and restructure chunk decode as async/worker-based per F12.
5. Feed the packed buffer into =jessie='s =rothermel.wgsl= to close the
   loop on Plan C's fire-behavior question.
6. Add LZW for older-TIFF coverage.
