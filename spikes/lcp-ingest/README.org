#+TITLE: Plan C Spike — Cost of Loading GeoTIFF / LCP Landscapes
#+AUTHOR: RJ Sheperd
#+DATE: 2026-10-15

Scope: the *ingest* question only — what does it cost to get FlamMap
landscape data from a file into a GPU-ready buffer, without GDAL? The
fire-behavior comparison half of Plan C ([[file:../../FLAMMAP_WEBGPU_PLANS.org][FLAMMAP_WEBGPU_PLANS.org]] §5) is
not addressed here.

Answer: *~190 ms single-threaded for a 4096² landscape* (16.8M cells,
~123 km square at 30 m), producing 128 MB of packed cells. Ingest is not
the bottleneck, and no GDAL is required.

* Files

| =lcp.mjs= | Dependency-free reader: raw =.lcp=, LCP-as-GeoTIFF (stripped + tiled), deinterleave, 8 B/cell packing. Runs in browser or bun. |
| =bench.mjs= | Timing harness, median of 7, with projections. |
| =gen.mjs= | Synthetic landscape generator, so scaling is measured rather than extrapolated. |

#+BEGIN_SRC shell
bun run gen.mjs /tmp/lcp 4096 4096 --tiled --raw
bun run bench.mjs /tmp/lcp/synth_4096x4096_tile.tif
bun run bench.mjs ../../SpatialFOFEM/SampleData/Landscape/BlueMountain_LCP_FCCS.tif
#+END_SRC

* Measurements

Apple Silicon, bun 1.1.38, median of 7, single-threaded. Real sample is
=SpatialFOFEM/SampleData/Landscape/BlueMountain_LCP_FCCS.tif=; the rest are
synthetic 10-band landscapes.

| Grid | Cells | Parse (ms) | Fused pack (ms) | *Total (ms)* | Packed | Deflated |
|---+---+---+---+---+---+---|
| 482 x 554 (real) | 267 K | 0.71 | 2.28 | *2.99* | 2.0 MB | 0.56 MB (3.64:1) |
| 1024² | 1.05 M | 3.56 | 8.49 | *12.05* | 8.0 MB | 1.98 MB (4.05:1) |
| 2048² | 4.19 M | 18.46 | 41.36 | *59.82* | 32 MB | 8.21 MB (3.90:1) |
| 4096² | 16.8 M | 60.43 | 129.44 | *189.88* | 128 MB | 44.0 MB (2.91:1) |

Throughput settles at roughly *130 M cells/s* for the fused pack and
*280 M cells/s* for TIFF assembly.

* Findings

** F1 — Ingest is cheap enough to ignore, and GDAL is unnecessary

190 ms for a 4096² landscape, against =jessie='s 74 ms of GPU simulation
and ~950 ms end-to-end. Ingest is a minor term. More importantly the whole
reader is *~300 lines with no dependencies* — confirming
ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW S3b.6a that GDAL-in-WASM (10–20 MB of payload) can be
avoided on the input path entirely.

** F2 — Fusing deinterleave into packing is a free 2x

The obvious two-pass structure (deinterleave to per-band planes, then pack)
costs 405 ms at 4096². Writing the packed layout *directly* from the
pixel-interleaved samples costs 190 ms — *2.13x faster, byte-identical
output*, and it avoids allocating ~335 MB of intermediate planes.

Per-band planes are never needed if the consumer is the GPU. Keep
=deinterleave()= for debugging and validation only.

** F3 — The brief's 5:1 compression assumption is too optimistic

=wildfire-sim-research.org= flags this as a TODO ("the 5:1 figure is
assumed"). Measured deflate on *packed* cells:

| 1024² | 4.05:1 |
| 2048² | 3.90:1 |
| 4096² | *2.91:1* |

The ratio *degrades with area*, because packing has already removed the
redundancy that compression would otherwise find. ZSTD would do somewhat
better than deflate, but *5:1 should not be used for sizing*. At 2.9:1 a
4096² window is ~44 MB on the wire, not ~26 MB — i.e. ~3.5 s at 100 Mbit/s
rather than 2.0 s.

Caveat: synthetic terrain. Re-measure on real LANDFIRE windows before
freezing any budget.

** F4 — TIFF sample data is NOT safely castable to Int16Array

Strip and tile offsets are arbitrary byte offsets and are routinely *odd* —
the real BlueMountain file puts its first strip at byte 5049.
=new Int16Array(buffer, 5049)= throws =RangeError: Byte offset is not
aligned=. So zero-copy int16 views over TIFF payloads are not generally
possible; one byte-wise copy per strip/tile into an aligned destination is
unavoidable.

This cost ~20 minutes of spike time and would have cost the same in any
implementation. It is the single most likely thing to surprise someone
writing this reader fresh.

** F5 — Tiled vs stripped does not matter for whole-file reads

At 1024², stripped 21.70 ms vs tiled 21.74 ms. Tiling matters for *HTTP
range requests* (fetching a window without the whole file), not for local
parse throughput. The research brief's tiling guidance (ADR-01, 512²
tiles) is about network access patterns, and this spike does not exercise
that — it measures the local path only.

** F6 — Band count must come from the right source, per format

A raw =.lcp= implies its band count from the header's =CrownFuels= /
=GroundFuels= flags (=21= = present): 5 basic + 3 crown + 2 ground = 10.
But the real =BlueMountain_LCP_FCCS.tif= has *9* bands, which no
combination of those flags produces — it is an FCCS variant that does not
follow strict LCP banding.

So: *trust =SamplesPerPixel= for GeoTIFF, trust the flags only for raw
=.lcp=.* A reader that assumes 10 bands, or that applies the LCP flag
convention to a GeoTIFF, will stride incorrectly and silently produce
garbage.

** F7 — The 7,316-byte LCP header is confirmed, and must be parsed field-wise

Summing =headdata= (=firemodels/lib/flammap/include/flammap3.h:190-269=)
gives *exactly 7,316 bytes*, matching the research brief. Composition: 3
longs + 4 doubles + 10 themes x 412 B + 2 longs + 4 doubles + 1 long +
2 doubles + 10 shorts + 10 x 256 B filenames + 512 B description.

But the equivalent C struct would be *7,320* bytes under natural alignment
— a =double= at offset 12 forces 4 bytes of padding after the three
leading longs. *The on-disk header is unpadded*, so it must be read
field-by-field; a packed-struct memcpy will be off by 4 bytes from the
first double onward.

** F8 — Scaling is mildly super-linear

Linear extrapolation from 1024² predicts 193 ms at 4096²; measured 190 ms
for the fused path, but the two-pass path degrades (365 ms predicted vs
405 ms measured, ~1.1x) and per-stage throughput drops as the working set
leaves cache (deinterleave 82 → 74 M cells/s from 1024² to 4096²).
Projections beyond 8192² should be treated as optimistic floors.

* Correctness

=gen.mjs= writes the same synthetic landscape three ways — raw =.lcp=,
stripped GeoTIFF, tiled GeoTIFF — and all three readers produce
*byte-identical* interleaved output. The generator deliberately places
sample data at an odd offset to reproduce F4.

The fused and two-pass packing paths are asserted byte-identical in
=bench.mjs=. (They initially differed: one rounded canopy height, the other
truncated. Quantization rounding must match exactly between any two paths
that are meant to agree — a small instance of the general risk in
ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW Q0.7.)

* What this spike does NOT establish

- *No browser measurement.* All timings are bun on the dev machine. Browser
  typed-array performance should be comparable but is unconfirmed, and
  =jessie='s harness (Playwright + =test_stress.mjs=) is the place to
  confirm it.
- *No HTTP/range-request path.* F5 explains why that is the interesting
  case for large landscapes, and it is untested here. The brief's COG
  profile, OPFS caching, and Zarr weather streaming are all unexercised.
- *No compressed TIFF.* The reader rejects anything but
  =Compression=1=. Real LANDFIRE COGs are DEFLATE or ZSTD, so a decoder is
  required before this handles production data. That is the obvious next
  increment and it will shift the cost balance toward decode.
- *No fire behavior.* Packing is validated for self-consistency, not
  against FlamMap. Whether these quantized inputs produce acceptable fire
  behavior is the other half of Plan C.
- *Synthetic compression ratios.* See F3's caveat.

* Recommended next steps

1. Add a DEFLATE/ZSTD decoder path (=DecompressionStream= for deflate,
   ZSTD-WASM otherwise) and re-measure — this is what real LANDFIRE data
   needs, and decode will likely dominate ingest afterwards.
2. Port =bench.mjs= into =jessie='s Playwright harness for in-browser
   numbers.
3. Re-run F3 against a real LANDFIRE window rather than synthetic terrain.
4. Feed the packed buffer into =jessie='s =rothermel.wgsl= to close the
   loop on Plan C's fire-behavior question.
